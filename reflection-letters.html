Being Seen · Reflection 06

The letter that took me back to 1986

1986. My sister had just been born, and my grandma flew over to help my mum through those first weeks. We'd just bought our very first hi-fi from Tottenham Court Road, because back then that was the place to go.

The first cassette Grandma put in was Chitthi Aayi Hai. Within moments, she was in tears.

I was just a boy, and I asked her why she was crying. She told me that when she left South Africa for India, she left everyone behind: her family, her brothers and sisters, everything she knew. She was alone. The only thing that kept her going was waiting for a letter, any news at all from home.

Years later, my mum did the same. She left India for England and lived for those blue airmail letters from her parents. She'd read them again and again.

Two generations of women, two journeys, one song.

This week, a handwritten letter arrived for me. This one wasn't from family. It came from a patient who sat down and wrote, pen on paper, every word considered.

And as I read it, I was back in that living room in 1986, watching my grandma cry.

A handwritten note says "I thought about you." That's what being seen really means.

To the patient who wrote it: thank you. This one means more than you know.

Who remembers receiving letters?
