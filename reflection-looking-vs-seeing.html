Being Seen · Reflection 02

Looking and seeing are not the same thing

My profession is built on looking. Lights, lenses, magnification. We can examine the front of the eye in remarkable detail.

But the instruments only tell half the story. The other half is sitting in the chair: a person who may be worried, tired, embarrassed about a symptom, or simply hoping someone will take them seriously.

You can look at someone for twenty minutes and never see them. And you can see someone in a single moment: the pause before they answer, the question they almost didn't ask.

I've come to believe that seeing is a choice. It means slowing down, putting the agenda aside for a breath, and being curious about the person rather than just the problem.

It's true in clinic. It's just as true at the dinner table, in a team meeting, or in a five-minute conversation with a stranger.

This week, who might you be looking at without truly seeing?
