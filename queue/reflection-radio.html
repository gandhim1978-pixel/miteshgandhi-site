Turning Points · 02

From the quiet son to live on BBC Radio

“On air in 3… 2… 1…”

BBC Radio Cambridgeshire. 2019. Live.

And the voice going out across the county was mine.

If you'd told my younger self this, he'd have laughed. Then quietly changed the subject.

I grew up deferring to a strong father. I didn't leave home until my twenties. I was the one who listened, never the one who spoke.

So what changed?

Not one big leap. A staircase of small, frightening steps.

Saying yes to a stranger's house. Picking up a microphone at a wedding. Standing in front of students at Anglia Ruskin. Walking into Clamp one day a week, and staying.

Each step terrified me. Each one made the next a little lighter.

There's something I teach about the eye that I only understood about myself much later.

To focus on something close, the eye's lens tightens. To see into the distance, it has to relax.

For years, I clenched. I gripped tightly to what was safe, what was near, what I knew. And my world stayed small.

It was only when I learned to soften, to trust, to let go a little, that I could see further.

The stillness I first found on a mountain in India was still there that morning, quietly underneath the nerves. It always had been.

And here's the truth I hold closest: I never climbed that staircase alone.

My parents' sacrifice. My wife's belief. My colleagues' trust. My patients' kindness. And a grace I can't fully explain, which has carried me through every step I thought I couldn't take.

I didn't find courage, then act. I acted, and courage followed.

It still does. Every single time.
