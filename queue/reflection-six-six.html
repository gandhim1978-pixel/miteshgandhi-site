Success isn't 6/6

In the consulting room, there is a number every optometrist knows by heart.

6/6.

It means you can see at six metres what a typical eye sees at six metres. It's the line on the chart we all aim for. The box we tick. The definition of good vision.

And for a long time, I thought it was the definition of a job well done.

It isn't.

You can reach 6/6 and still send someone home unhappy. Their eyes may be uncomfortable. Their lenses may feel strange. They may not have understood a word you said. They may simply have felt rushed.

The chart measures what the eye can see. It doesn't measure what the person feels.

In my specialist contact lens work, I learned this lesson again and again. Success is not the letters on the wall. Success is the moment someone blinks, looks up and says, “That's it. That feels right.”

Science gives us acuity. It's precise, measurable, essential. I wouldn't practise without it.

But there is another kind of seeing. Not the eye taking in light, but one person truly noticing another.

Vision belongs to the eye. Perception belongs to the heart.

I've come to believe the best clinicians, the best leaders, the best friends, are the ones who measure both.

When did someone last make you feel truly seen?
