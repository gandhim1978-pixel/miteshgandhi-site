Turning Points · 08

One day a week

Two single days a week. That's all it was.

In 2017, several things in my life went wrong at once. It was one of the hardest years I have known.

Circumstances meant I had to look for work in new places. I didn't feel brave. I felt unsettled, uncertain, a little lost.

So I did the only thing I could. I took the small opportunities in front of me.

One day a week at an independent practice in Cambridge called Clamp. And time teaching students at Anglia Ruskin University.

Neither felt like a grand plan. They felt like stepping stones laid across a fast river.

But teaching connected me with new people. New people opened new doors. And at Clamp, I quietly did my best work, one patient at a time.

Then something unexpected happened. The practice's director decided to move on.

And because I was already there, already known, already trusted, I was offered the role.

By 2018, I was Clinical Director, alongside my partner David.

People sometimes call that luck. I see it differently. The door opened, but I was only standing in front of it because I'd taken those two small, uncertain steps.

Looking back, the hardest year of my life was quietly preparing the best years of my life.

What small step are you underestimating right now?
