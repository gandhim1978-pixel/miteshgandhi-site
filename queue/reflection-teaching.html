Teaching taught me more

Since 2016, I have taught optometry students at Anglia Ruskin University.

I walked in expecting to share what I knew. I walked out, week after week, having learned more than I taught.

Students ask the questions experienced clinicians have stopped asking. Why do we do it this way? What if the patient doesn't understand? What if you're wrong?

Those questions keep me honest.

Teaching also showed me how much of good care has nothing to do with equipment. It's how you greet someone. How you explain. How you listen when a patient is frightened.

Those are things that can't be learned from a textbook alone. They're learned by watching, practising and being gently corrected.

I also facilitate peer review for colleagues, small groups of optometrists discussing real cases together. It's humbling. There is always someone who sees something you missed.

I've come to believe that the moment you stop learning from others is the moment you stop growing.

Teaching isn't really about being the expert in the room. It's about being the most willing learner in it.

And perhaps that's true of life, too. Every person we meet is quietly teaching us something, if we're humble enough to notice.

Who has taught you something they never knew they taught?
