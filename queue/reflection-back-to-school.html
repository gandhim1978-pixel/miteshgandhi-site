Back to school at 42

Twenty years after qualifying, I sat my exams again.

In 2020, at the age of 42, I began training as an Independent Prescriber. It meant study, essays, clinical placements and exams, alongside running a practice and raising a young family.

There were evenings I wondered what I was doing.

I already had a career. I already had a title. Why go back to being a student?

Because I could see what it would mean for my patients.

As a prescribing optometrist, I can treat many eye conditions directly, without someone needing to wait for a hospital appointment. Fewer delays. Less worry. Care closer to home.

I completed my postgraduate certificate with a distinction. But the real reward came later, the first time I could say to an anxious patient, “We can treat this here, today.”

What surprised me most was how much I enjoyed being a beginner again. Not knowing. Asking questions. Feeling the small thrill of understanding something new.

We often think of learning as something for the young. I think it's something for the alive.

The moment we decide we've learned enough, we begin to shrink.

So this new year, I'm not making resolutions. I'm making a promise to stay curious.

What would you love to learn, if age didn't matter?
