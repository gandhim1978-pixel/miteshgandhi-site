Turning Points · 03

“Just keep taking ibuprofen”

Every test came back normal. Every doctor said I was fine. Every part of me said I wasn't.

It was 2000. I had just qualified as an optometrist. A new career. A bright future. Everything I'd worked for.

Then a virus I couldn't name began to affect my heart.

I was tired in a way sleep didn't fix. Unsettled in a way I couldn't describe.

The advice was kind, and it was simple: “Just keep taking ibuprofen. You'll be fine.”

I took the tablets. I waited to feel fine. It didn't come.

And slowly, I realised the problem wasn't only in my body.

I was still living at home. Still going through the motions. Still waiting for permission to become myself. Nothing around me could nourish what was missing inside me.

So I stopped waiting.

I couldn't commit to a full-time job, because I didn't know what my health would allow next month. So I became a locum. Flexible. Uncertain. Free.

And I started searching, not outward this time, but inward.

That search led me to India. To meditation. To a stillness I didn't know existed.

It didn't replace medicine. But it gave me something medicine couldn't: steadiness while I healed, and clarity about the life I was meant to live.

Here's something I teach my students about the eye.

In bright light, the pupil shrinks. In darkness, it opens wide, to let in every bit of light it can find.

I think we're built the same way. When life is comfortable, we close down a little. It's in the dark that we finally open.

That illness was one of the darkest seasons of my life. It was also the moment I let the light in.

Today, when a patient sits in my chair and says, “Everything looks fine, but something isn't right,” I listen.

Because I've been that person.

And sometimes the quietest voice in the room is the one telling the truth.

When did you last trust a feeling nobody else could see?
