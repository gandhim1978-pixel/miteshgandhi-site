Turning Points · 08

The two years that rerouted my life

This is the hardest reflection I've written.

In 2017, a complaint was made about me to the General Optical Council, the body that regulates my profession.

For an optometrist, few things are more frightening.

It came from a moment of misjudgement on my part. I shared something personal, my own meditation practice, with a young person in a way that didn't belong in a professional setting. My intentions were kind. But intentions aren't the point. It was unprofessional, and I own that.

What followed was around two years of waiting.

Two years of letters, questions and uncertainty. Two years of lying awake, wondering whether the career I'd spent my life building would survive.

In the end, no action was taken. Nothing was recorded against my name.

But I wasn't the same person who had walked into it.

During that time, I could no longer work where I had worked for years. The comfortable job, the familiar routine, the short drive home: all of it was gone.

So I had to look elsewhere.

I took one day a week at a small independent practice in Cambridge called Clamp. I took on more teaching at Anglia Ruskin University. I met new people. I learned new ways of working.

I didn't choose any of it bravely. I was pushed.

And yet those two years are the reason my life looks the way it does today. Within a year, I was a director at Clamp. Awards, teaching, prescribing, leadership: none of it would have happened if I'd stayed where I was comfortable.

I've learned three things.

First, owning your mistakes is a kind of freedom. The moment I stopped defending myself, I could start learning.

Second, I now keep my clinical life and my spiritual life as two separate doors. My patients come to me for their eyes, and that trust deserves protecting. My meditation is offered elsewhere, freely, to anyone who wants to find it.

Third, some of the experiences we fear most are the ones that redirect us towards our purpose.

In the eye, light only comes into focus after it has been bent. Those two years bent me. They also brought my life into focus.

What experience that once frightened you has quietly redirected your life?
