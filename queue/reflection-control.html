Turning Points · 13

The only thing I can control

Some days, work is the calmest place in my life.

At the practice, things make sense. A patient comes in with a problem. We investigate, we explain, we treat. There's a plan, and the plan usually works.

At home, life doesn't follow plans.

People I love are facing illnesses I can't cure. Some of them are carrying burdens I can't lift for them. My children are growing into their own people, making their own choices.

For someone who has spent his life fixing things, that is a hard lesson.

I'm an optometrist. I'm used to finding the cause, choosing the lens, solving the problem. But there's no prescription for watching someone you love struggle.

For a while, I threw myself into work, because work was the one place I could still make a difference. I suspect many of you know that feeling.

Slowly, I've learned something different.

I can't control what happens to the people I love. But I can control how present I am with them. Whether I listen. Whether I'm patient. Whether I turn up, again and again, even when there's nothing to fix.

In meditation, we practise letting go: not giving up, but releasing our grip on what was never ours to hold.

It's the hardest practice I know.

The eye can't focus on everything at once. It chooses one point, and lets the rest soften.

Perhaps that's what love asks of us in difficult seasons. Not to fix everything. Just to focus on being there.

What are you trying to control that is asking only for your presence?
