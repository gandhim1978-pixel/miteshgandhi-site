Going through the motions

From the outside, my early twenties looked fine.

I'd qualified as an optometrist. I had a profession, a family, a future.

On the inside, I was going through the motions.

I lived at home. I deferred to everyone. I did what was expected. And quietly, I wasn't at peace with myself.

I didn't talk about it. Men of my background often didn't. You worked hard, you kept your head down, you got on with it.

Then my body forced the question. An illness nobody could explain stopped me in my tracks. And with it came the realisation that something deeper was wrong.

I wasn't living my life. I was living a life.

I'm sharing this because I know how many people are quietly going through the motions right now. Successful on paper. Smiling at work. Empty somewhere inside.

If that's you, please hear this: it doesn't have to stay that way.

For me, the way forward came through travel, meditation, independence and, eventually, the people who loved me enough to push me. For others, it might be a conversation with a friend, a doctor or a counsellor. There's no shame in any of it.

The first step is simply admitting that you're not fine.

That's not weakness. It's the beginning of honesty.

If you stopped going through the motions, what would you want your life to feel like?
