The Inner Journey · 03

The endless search for more

There's always another achievement waiting to make us happy. So why does satisfaction stay just out of reach?

I've celebrated some wonderful milestones. Becoming a director. Awards for our practice. Qualifying as an Independent Prescriber with a distinction.

Each brought real joy. And each time, almost before the celebration ended, another target quietly appeared.

Ambition isn't wrong. Building a practice, caring for patients and providing for a family all require effort, and I'm grateful for every opportunity I've had.

I've also known the opposite. For years, I settled into a comfortable job that asked very little of me. That wasn't contentment either. It was simply standing still.

The question I keep returning to is this: can we strive without becoming permanently dissatisfied?

In Sahaja Yoga, the Nabhi is associated with contentment, generosity and balance between family, work and spirit.

That balance matters to me. My parents crossed continents with very little so their children could have more. I don't want to spend the “more” chasing yet more, while missing the people sitting right beside me.

Contentment isn't complacency. It's appreciating today while still working towards tomorrow.

Meditation gives me a moment to notice desire, rather than automatically obey it.

Perhaps the richest life isn't the one where we own everything. It's the one where we can finally see what we already have.
