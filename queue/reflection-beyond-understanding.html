The Inner Journey · 08

Beyond the need to understand everything

What if peace begins when we stop demanding an answer to every question?

My profession is built on understanding. Measurements, scans, evidence. I wouldn't practise any other way.

But in 2000, soon after qualifying, I became unwell, and nobody could tell me why. Every test came back normal. All my training, all that science, and no answer.

It was one of the most unsettling times of my life. It was also what sent me searching.

On a mountain in India, I experienced something I couldn't explain and didn't need to. A cool stillness. A sense, for the first time in a long time, that I was more than my worries.

In Sahaja Yoga, the Sahasrara is the opening to a higher awareness. Shri Mataji taught that self-realisation isn't an idea to believe, but an experience to discover.

This isn't an invitation to stop thinking. Meditation never replaced medicine for me. It gave me something alongside it: steadiness while I healed, and the discovery that I am more than my thoughts.

We don't have to understand everything before we can be still. Sometimes we have to be still before we can see anything clearly.

Information isn't wisdom. Analysis isn't peace.

The mind can describe peace. Meditation invites us to experience it.
