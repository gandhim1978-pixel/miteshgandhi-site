The Inner Journey · 09

We were never meant to grow alone

Can we truly grow spiritually if it makes us less compassionate towards others?

It's tempting to treat spirituality as a private achievement. My meditation. My progress. My ascent.

But I learned early that it doesn't work that way.

When I first returned from India and moved to St Neots, I found others drawn to the same stillness, and we began meeting to meditate together. Strangers became friends. That small circle helped me through some uncertain years.

I see the same truth at work. Every award our practice has won belongs to a team: people covering for each other, staying late, making nervous patients smile. No single person can replace a team that genuinely cares.

And every Navratri, I watch the Garba circles turning, hundreds of people moving in rhythm together. Nobody dances it alone.

In Sahaja Yoga, collectivity is central to spiritual growth. It doesn't mean losing our individuality or agreeing with everyone. It means recognising our shared humanity, and contributing to something larger than ourselves.

If our spirituality makes us feel superior to others, we may need to look again at what we're cultivating.

The real question isn't how spiritual I feel. It's whether I'm easier to live and work with.
