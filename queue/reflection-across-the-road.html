Turning Points · 16

Across the road

My parents live across the road from us now.

Two years ago, I encouraged them to move up to St Neots, so I could be close by and help more easily.

For a man who once believed he couldn't survive without them, there's something quietly extraordinary about that.

As a young man, I didn't leave home until my twenties. My parents did everything for me. I deferred, I depended, I stayed comfortable.

Now the roles have turned.

I'm the one popping over with shopping. The one sorting out appointments and paperwork. The one checking that all is well.

Full circle.

I won't pretend it's always easy. Caring for ageing parents brings its own worries, frustrations and tiredness. Anyone who has done it will know.

But there are gifts too. Cups of tea at the kitchen table. Old stories I'd never heard. My sons spending time with their grandparents, just across the road.

My parents crossed continents to build a life for their children. They gave up comfort, certainty and familiarity so we could have more.

Being able to give back, even a little, feels like the most natural thing in the world.

The boy who couldn't leave home has become the son who brought them home.

Who once carried you, whom you now have the chance to carry?
