The freedom of forgiveness

How much of our present are we giving away to something that happened in the past?

Most of us carry an archive of old hurts. A conversation that went wrong. A moment we felt misunderstood. A decision that wasn't fair.

I've carried my share. There were years when I replayed certain moments far more often than they deserved.

In Sahaja Yoga, the Agnya is the centre of forgiveness and humility. Its simplest affirmation is just two words: “I forgive.”

Readers of an earlier reflection may remember that, according to Sahaja Yoga, the Agnya sits where the nerve pathways from our two eyes meet and cross, the optic chiasm. As an optometrist, I find that quietly moving. The place where our two ways of seeing become one is also the place of forgiveness.

Forgiveness doesn't mean pretending hurtful behaviour was acceptable. It doesn't mean abandoning boundaries, or trusting someone who keeps causing harm.

It means recognising that resentment keeps our attention imprisoned in the very experience we want to leave behind.

We can also be unforgiving towards ourselves, judging our younger selves with wisdom we only gained later. I've had to learn to forgive the boy who stayed too long in his comfort zone.

Sometimes forgiveness begins as a decision, long before it becomes a feeling.

It doesn't rewrite the past. It changes the hold the past has on us.
