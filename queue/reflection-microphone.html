Turning Points · 04

I held the microphone and met my future

New Zealand. 2005.

My cousin's wedding. And I'd been asked to be the MC.

A few years earlier, the quiet boy who never left home would have found a polite reason to say no.

But something in me had shifted. So I picked up the microphone.

I welcomed the guests. I told the stories. I kept the evening flowing.

And somewhere in that room was a family friend's family. That's how I met Liril.

What followed was a love story across two hemispheres. Meeting halfway in Thailand. Counting the days between visits.

Within a year, we were engaged. Then married.

She left her country to build a life with me in a very small flat in St Neots.

And here's the part people don't see.

Every human eye has a blind spot. A small patch where we simply cannot see. The brain fills it in so cleverly that most of us never know it's there.

Our self-belief has one too. For years, mine sat right where my potential was.

Liril saw straight into it.

She didn't just marry the man I was. She believed in the man I could become.

When I settled for comfortable, she gently asked for more. When I doubted myself, she simply didn't.

I often think about that evening. How many quiet threads had to be woven for two people from opposite ends of the earth to end up in the same room?

I don't believe that was chance. I believe I was being guided, long before I knew how to notice it.

And I'm grateful every single day that I didn't stay in my seat.

Step up when you're asked. You never know who's in the room.

Who saw something in you before you could see it yourself?
