Waiting for the eyes to adjust

Walk from a bright room into darkness, and at first you see nothing.

Then, slowly, shapes appear. The edge of a door. A chair. A window.

This is called dark adaptation. The cells at the back of the eye that work best in low light, called rods, need time to become fully sensitive. It can take twenty minutes or more before your eyes reach their best night vision.

The darkness hasn't changed. Your eyes have.

I've been thinking about this a lot lately.

Some seasons of life feel like walking into a dark room. Illness in the family. Worry about the people we love. Changes we didn't ask for.

At first, we see nothing. No way forward. No sense of what to do.

The temptation is to panic, to grab for the light switch, to demand clarity immediately.

But the eye teaches patience.

If we can stay in the dark a little longer, without rushing, without fighting it, our vision begins to adjust. Shapes emerge. A path appears. Small sources of light we'd never noticed become visible.

In meditation, we practise exactly this. Sitting in uncertainty. Letting the inner eye adjust.

I can't promise the darkness passes quickly. But I've learned that we see more in it than we expect, if we give ourselves time.

What might you see if you gave your eyes a little longer to adjust?
