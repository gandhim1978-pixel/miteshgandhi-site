Turning Points · 06

The year I learned to be alone

The first night in someone else's house, I didn't know where anything was.

The light switches. The kettle. Which drawer held the spoons.

It was 2001. I had met a woman on a mountain in India who lived in a town called St Neots. Her husband had been posted to Hong Kong, and she'd asked if I would look after their home.

I had said yes. And now here I was, in my twenties, living away from my parents for the very first time.

Nobody to cook for me. Nobody to decide for me. Nobody to fall back on.

It was frightening. And it was the most important year of my life.

I learned to shop, to budget, to fix things, to sit with my own company. I kept working as a locum, driving to practices across the area.

And in the evenings, I meditated.

I found others nearby who were drawn to the same stillness, and we began meeting to meditate together. Strangers became friends. A borrowed house became a home.

For years I had believed independence would feel like loneliness.

It didn't. It felt like meeting myself for the first time.

That town is still my home, a quarter of a century later.

Sometimes the life we're meant to live is waiting just outside the door we're most afraid to open.
