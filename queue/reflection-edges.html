The edges of our vision

Hold your gaze on a single word on this page.

Without moving your eyes, notice what's around it. The edges of the screen. Movement nearby. Light and shape at the corners of your vision.

That's your peripheral vision. It isn't sharp. It can't read or recognise faces well. But it's remarkably good at noticing movement, which is why you catch something moving at the corner of your eye before you can say what it is.

Our central vision gives us detail. Our peripheral vision gives us awareness.

I think most of us live too much in our central vision.

We focus on the task, the target, the screen in front of us. And we miss what's happening at the edges: the colleague who's gone quiet, the friend who's stopped calling, the parent who's slowing down, the child who needs a moment of our time.

Some of the most important things in life never arrive in the centre of our attention. They arrive at the edges.

In the practice, I try to train myself to notice the edges. The patient whose hands are shaking. The relative waiting anxiously outside. The team member who's having a hard day.

At home, I'm still learning.

Meditation helps. In stillness, attention widens. We become aware not only of what we're looking at, but of everything around it.

Who is at the edge of your vision right now, waiting to be noticed?
