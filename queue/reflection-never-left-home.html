Turning Points · 05

The boy who never left home

Not for university. Not for my first job. Not once.

I grew up in London, the son of parents who had crossed continents to give us a future. They had known real hardship, and they did everything they could to shield us from it.

They cooked. They organised. They decided. And I let them.

My father was a strong man, shaped by a childhood that asked a great deal of him. In his house, I learned to listen, to defer, to keep the peace.

I studied optometry at City University and travelled home every evening. I qualified. I had a profession, a future, a respectable path.

And quietly, without ever saying it out loud, I believed I couldn't survive on my own.

Comfort is a strange thing. It doesn't feel like a cage. It feels like love, like safety, like home.

But a bird that is always fed never learns to fly.

I wasn't unhappy, exactly. I was going through the motions. Living a life that worked, without feeling that it was mine.

It would take an illness, a journey to India and a stranger's spare key before I finally stepped outside that door.

I share this not as a complaint. My parents gave me everything they had. But I've come to believe that the greatest gift we can give the people we love is not protection.

It's the confidence to leave.

What comfort in your life might quietly be holding you back?
