The Inner Journey · 05

Do we know how to love?

Our closest relationships often teach us our greatest lessons about love.

I met my wife at a wedding in New Zealand, and we built our life together from a tiny flat in St Neots. She believed in me before I believed in myself.

We have two teenage sons. Being their father is the greatest privilege of my life, and the most humbling.

Family life brings enormous joy. It also exposes our expectations.

We want the people we love to understand us. We want our children to make good choices. We want our efforts to be noticed. Sometimes we wish our own parents had understood us better.

Today, my parents live across the road from us. The boy who once couldn't imagine leaving home is now the man they lean on. Love, I've found, keeps changing shape.

In Sahaja Yoga, the Anahata is the centre of the heart: unconditional love, compassion, courage and security.

The question I keep asking myself is simple. Do the people closest to me experience my love in the way I intend it?

Good intentions aren't always enough. Patience, listening, consistency and kindness matter too.

Loving someone doesn't mean demanding they become a copy of us. Nor does it mean accepting harmful behaviour without boundaries.

Love isn't only measured by how deeply we feel. It's revealed by how freely we let others be themselves.
