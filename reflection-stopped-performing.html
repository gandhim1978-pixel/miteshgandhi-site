Being Seen · Reflection 05

The day I stopped performing

After the performance. Photo: Pexels.

If you've read my story, you'll know about the vitiligo, so I won't retell it here.

But there was a turning point. I simply got tired of managing how everyone saw me. The adjusting, the covering, the careful angles. It was exhausting.

So I stopped.

Not dramatically. Not with some grand announcement. I just quietly decided: people who like me will like me. Those who don't, won't. And that's fine.

Something strange happened after that. I relaxed. And when I relaxed, people came closer. Real conversations started. Real friendships formed. Not because I'd changed anything about my skin, but because I'd stopped asking the world for permission to be myself.

In Sahaja meditation, the Vishuddhi chakra, the energy centre at the throat, is associated with self-expression and self-respect. In my own practice, I've come to see it like this: when it feels blocked, we shrink; when it opens, we stop performing and start simply being.

That's what happened to me. I stopped performing.

Being seen starts with allowing yourself to be seen. Not the polished version. The actual one.

What would change if you stopped hiding the thing you're most self-conscious about?
