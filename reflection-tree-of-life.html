Science and Spirit · 01

The tree of life in the eye

Retinal images are fully anonymised.

Every working day, I look into the back of the human eye.

And every day, at the centre of it all, I find the same small, glowing circle: the optic disc.

To an optometrist, it is a landmark. It is where more than a million nerve fibres gather and leave the eye, carrying everything we see to the brain. From its centre, the blood vessels emerge and branch outward, upwards and downwards, nourishing the whole retina.

One point. Then branching. Then life spreading outward.

After 26 years, it still stops me in my tracks.

Here is the part that moves me most. The optic disc has no light-sensing cells at all. It is our blind spot. The very place where all our sight leaves the eye is the one place where we cannot see.

The source of our vision is itself invisible to us.

I can't think of a more beautiful description of the spirit.

Follow those nerve fibres back from both eyes, and they meet behind the eyes at the optic chiasm. There, half the fibres from each eye cross over to the other side. It is, quite literally, a cross: the place where the two halves of our seeing become one.

In Sahaja Yoga, this is the seat of the Agnya chakra, the centre of forgiveness and of thoughtless awareness. Shri Mataji Nirmala Devi described the Tree of Life as being manifested on the cross of the optic chiasma within us.

When I first heard that, I was struck. As a clinician, I had been looking at that tree every day of my working life.

I want to be clear about one thing. I'm not making a clinical claim. The retina doesn't prove anything about the spirit, and I would never ask it to.

But I have come to believe that science and spirituality are two lenses on the same truth. Science shows me how the eye works. Meditation lets me wonder why it is so beautiful.

As above, so within. The patterns of creation repeat themselves, in a tree, in a river delta, in the vessels of a single eye.

The more closely I look, the more I see. And the more still I become, the more I perceive.
