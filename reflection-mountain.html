Turning Points · 01

A stranger on a mountain gave me the keys to my life

Ganpatipule, India, 2001.

I was 23. Sitting on a mountain in India. And for the first time in months, my mind was still.

I had never lived away from home. Not for university. Not for my first job. Not once.

My parents did everything for me. Somewhere along the way, I'd started to believe I couldn't survive without them.

Then a mystery virus attacked my heart. The doctors shrugged. “Just keep taking ibuprofen. You'll be fine.”

I wasn't fine.

Some part of me knew it. Not in my head. Deeper. A quiet knowing I couldn't explain, and couldn't ignore.

So I did the one thing I had never done. I left.

I went to India searching. For health. For meaning. For myself. I learned to sit in silence, and to listen to the quieter voice beneath the noise.

And on that mountain, I got talking to a stranger.

She lived in a small town in Cambridgeshire I'd barely heard of. St Neots. Her husband had just been offered a job in Hong Kong.

Then she asked a question that quietly changed the rest of my life: “Would you look after our house for a year?”

I said yes. No plan. No strategy. Just… yes.

She handed me the key to her home. Looking back, she handed me the key to my life.

That year, I learned who I was when nobody was looking after me.

25 years on, I still live a few miles from that house. And I've never stopped being grateful to her.

Here's what I've come to believe.

When we're lost, we stare at the horizon, waiting for a sign. But as an optometrist, I know the eye catches some of life's most important movements not in the centre, but at the edges.

Grace works the same way. It rarely arrives from straight ahead. It slips in from the corner of your vision. Dressed as a stranger. Disguised as an ordinary question.

Something larger than me was quietly arranging the pieces. All it asked of me was one small “yes”.
